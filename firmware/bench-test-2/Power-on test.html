Test 2: the light valve with power on

What this test checks. With no power, the valve blocks glare (that was test 1). With some power, it should let a screen's light through about evenly at every angle, so phones and laptops stay readable through the glasses whichever way you tilt your head. We call that screen mode. This test finds the voltage that gives it, by eye. It takes about 5 minutes.

How it works. The board sends the valve quick pulses that go one way, then the other way, a hundred times a second. Equal pulses both ways mean no steady current flows through the valve, which would slowly damage it. The slider on this page sets how strong the pulses are.

What you need

  • The QT Py plugged into this laptop, with the test 2 program on it. To put it on, double-click Install to board.bat in this folder once, and wait for it to say DONE.
  • The light valve you used in test 1 (one film peeled off), with its two wires connected to the board as in the picture.
  • The light sensor isn't needed. It can stay plugged in.

Get ready

  1. Set the screen brightness to full and turn Night light off.
  2. Dim the room lights a little, so the screen is the brightest thing you see.
  3. Press F11 to make this page full screen.

Keys

→ ←a little more or less power (0.05 volts)
↑ ↓a bigger step (0.2 volts)
0power off
Mmark this voltage as screen mode
Spacenext step
Escpower off and back to this screen

Wiring

  1. Unplug the QT Py's USB cable while you connect the wires.
  2. Connect one valve wire to the hole labelled A2 and the other to A3. Either way round works. Check the labels printed on the board.
  3. Make sure the two bare wire ends don't touch each other or any other hole.
  4. Plug the USB cable back in.

Why only A2 and A3: the board switches those two between pushing one way and the other. 3V, 5V and GND are always on, so they would push a steady current through the valve.
