Test 1: does the lens block glare with the power off?

What this test checks. Laptop screens give off polarized light: its light waves all wiggle in one direction, like glare bouncing off water or a road. A polarized lens blocks that light when it's turned one way, and lets it through when it's turned a quarter turn. In this test the screen plays the glare and the sensor plays your eye. You turn each sample slowly while the sensor measures how much light gets through.

The page does the rest. It changes the screen color, talks to the board, counts down, beeps, and shows the results. A full run takes about 13 minutes. First time? Press "Practice run": it only does your sunglasses on a white screen, about 2 minutes.

What you need

  • The small sensor board plugged into the QT Py with its cable, and the QT Py plugged into this laptop.
  • Polarized sunglasses. Check them first: look at this screen through them and slowly turn them. If the screen goes much darker at one angle, they're polarized. If nothing changes, they won't work for this test. If they're in a frame, fold the arms: only one lens goes on the screen.
  • Optional: a liquid crystal cell (the Adafruit light valve) with the plastic film peeled off one side. The peeled side is bare glass, and it goes against the screen. Leave its two wires unconnected: this test uses no power. Don't have one ready? Untick it below.
  • A marker pen or a small strip of tape.

Get ready

  1. Draw a short arrow near the edge of each sample, pointing outward like a clock hand. Keep it away from the middle, because the sensor looks through there. Any direction is fine.
  2. Turn the room lights off, so the screen is the only light. Room light sneaking under the sensor makes the glare numbers look worse than they are. No tube or paper is needed.
  3. Plug in the laptop charger, turn Night light off, and set the screen brightness to about three quarters.
  4. Tip the screen back as far as it goes and rest your elbows on the desk.
  5. Turn the sound on, and press F11 to make this page full screen.

How to hold it

Turn the lens so your arrow points at the thick highlighted line. Each time, turn it a little to the left (anticlockwise), toward the bigger number.

What your hands do

One hand holds the sensor board on the middle of the lens, chip side touching it. The other hand turns the lens by its edge. The sensor can turn along with the lens; that's fine. Press lightly: pressing hard makes colored blotches on the screen and spoils the readings.

Sounds and keys

3 ticksfinish moving, a measurement is about to start
1 beepmeasuring now, keep still
2 quick beepsdone, go to the next step
low buzzsomething needs fixing, read the top of the screen
2 rising notesall finished

Space I'm ready, go now. P pause the countdown (press again to carry on). "Skip this sample" moves on to the next sample. Esc stops the whole test; starting again begins from step 1.

What happens, in order

  1. Zero reading: your fingertip over the chip.
  2. 100% reading: the sensor straight on the screen, nothing in between.
  3. Sunglasses: 13 readings while you turn the lens half a turn, 15° at a time.
  4. Liquid crystal cell: the same.
  5. Steps 2 to 4 again on a red screen, then on a blue screen.
  6. Results, with PASS or FAIL and what they mean.
